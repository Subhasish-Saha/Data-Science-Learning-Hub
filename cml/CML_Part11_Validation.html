Part 11 of 17 · Model Selection & Validation

Producing a number you can defend Hold-out through k-fold, stratified, grouped and time-series splits; how k itself trades bias against variance; nested CV and the optimism it removes; grid versus random versus Bayesian search with the result that explains why; learning and validation curves as decision tools; per-slice error analysis; and the statistics of an honest model comparison.

Everything you report rests on this part, which is why it comes before metrics rather than after. The single most valuable idea is that a tuned CV score is a selection statistic, not an estimate — it is the maximum of many noisy numbers, so it is optimistically biased even if every setting were equally good. That is what nested CV exists to fix. The second is that an aggregate metric tells you whether something is wrong, and only slice analysis tells you what. And the third is the answer to “your model is 0.4% better — is it better?”, which has a statistical half, a multiple-comparisons half and a practical half, and needs all three.
