3.1 RNN mechanics

One weight matrix, applied over and over. That repetition is where the power comes from — and where all three limitations come from too.

The recurrence: h_t = tanh(W_x x_t + W_h h_{t-1} + b), output y_t = g(W_y h_t). The hidden state h_t is a compressed summary of everything seen so far, and the same weights are shared across every timestep — which is why an RNN can handle variable-length input at all.

3.2 Vanishing and exploding gradients ⭐

Same cause, opposite symptoms, different fixes. Candidates who lump them together lose the follow-up.

3.3 LSTM ⭐

Guaranteed to come up. The question is never “what is an LSTM” — it's “walk me through the gates” and “how exactly does the cell state fix vanishing gradients?”

Say it like this

“A vanilla RNN's gradient is multiplied by the same weight matrix at every step, so it shrinks or grows geometrically. The LSTM adds a separate cell state that is updated additively and gated elementwise — so there is a path along which the gradient is multiplied only by the forget gate. With the forget gate near 1, that path preserves gradient magnitude over hundreds of steps. It's called the constant error carousel.”

3.4 GRU

Two gates instead of three, one state instead of two. The honest answer to “which is better” is not a preference.

3.5 Bidirectional and deep RNNs

A one-line architecture change with a hard constraint attached — and the constraint is the interview question.

A BiRNN/BiLSTM runs one pass left-to-right and one right-to-left, then concatenates the states, so each position sees the full sentence. That is legal only when the whole sequence is available up front.

Bidirectional is…Because
✅ Fine for POS, NER, classification, extractive QAThe full input exists before you predict anything
❌ Impossible for autoregressive generationThe “future” tokens are what you're trying to produce — reading them is label leakage
❌ Impossible for true streaming/incremental decodingYou'd have to wait for the end of the utterance

Depth: stack layers, add residual connections between them, and place dropout carefully — standard dropout on the recurrent connection destroys memory, so use variational (same mask across timesteps) or apply dropout only to the feedforward direction. BiLSTM-CRF was the pre-BERT state of the art for NER and POS, and remains a legitimate answer when your compute budget is a CPU.

3.7 Attention before transformers ⭐

The direct ancestor of everything in Part 4. Understand it here, in its simplest form, and self-attention becomes obvious later.

The fix is almost embarrassingly direct: stop compressing. Let the decoder look at all encoder states, weighted differently at each output step. c_t = Σ_i α_{t,i} h_i where α = softmax(score(s_t, h_i)).

3.8 Would you ship an RNN in 2026?

A test of whether you can hold two ideas at once: the architecture lost, and its core idea came back.

SituationVerdict
Text classification, NER, QA, generation❌ A transformer encoder or decoder wins on every axis
Tiny model on a microcontroller / embedded device✅ Plausible — small, no attention memory
Strict streaming with O(1) state per step✅ Recurrence is structurally the right shape
Extremely long sequences where quadratic attention is prohibitive✅ …but in the modern form: an SSM, not an LSTM
The answer that shows range

“Rarely for text — but the concepts didn't die. A fixed-size recurrent state updated per token, with O(1) memory at inference, is exactly what Mamba and the SSM family revived, for exactly the reason RNNs were invented: attention's cost grows with sequence length and a recurrence's does not. The 2026 answer isn't ‘RNNs are obsolete’, it's ‘recurrence came back wearing better maths’.” (Detail in Part 5, §5.4.)

3.11 WordPiece

BPE with a better merge criterion. One formula separates them, and knowing it is a cheap way to sound precise.

Used by BERT, DistilBERT and ELECTRA. Instead of merging the most frequent pair, WordPiece merges the pair that most increases the likelihood of the training data — which in practice scores candidates by

The one formula

score(A,B) = freq(AB) / (freq(A) × freq(B))

A pair only wins if it occurs together more than its parts' individual frequencies would predict. So WordPiece prefers merges that are informative, while BPE prefers merges that are merely common — a very frequent character next to a very frequent character gets penalised.

Other mechanics: continuation pieces are marked ## (token, ##ization), and encoding is greedy longest-match-first from the left rather than a merge replay. Text is pre-split on whitespace and punctuation, so it is not directly usable for languages without spaces.

3.15 Practicalities and label alignment

The engineering half. One of these is the single most common practical NLP bug.

Currency check — transformers 5.x

The slow vs fast tokenizer distinction was removed in transformers v5. AutoTokenizer now always returns the Rust-backed implementation, and use_fast= is history. If you say “I'd load the slow tokenizer to inspect offsets” you are describing a library that no longer exists — the correct answer is return_offsets_mapping=True and word_ids(). Current release: 5.15.0 (10 Aug 2026), Python 3.10–3.14, PyTorch only.

3.16 Interview focus for Part 3

Nine questions. The tokenization ones are the highest-yield in the whole subject relative to how little most candidates prepare for them.

Sequences & tokenization · 9 questions
How does an RNN work, and what are its real limitations?

h_t = tanh(W_x x_t + W_h h_{t-1} + b) — a hidden state carrying a compressed summary of the prefix, with the same weights applied at every timestep. Trained with backpropagation through time.

Four limitations, precisely: (1) vanishing/exploding gradients, because the gradient through k steps carries a factor of W_h^k; (2) no parallelism over time — step t needs step t−1, so training can't be parallelised along the sequence, which is the property transformers exploit; (3) degraded long-range memory even with gating; (4) in seq2seq, the fixed-size context vector bottleneck.

Vanishing vs exploding gradients — same problem?

Same cause, opposite symptoms, different fixes. Both come from repeatedly multiplying by W_h: if the spectral radius is below 1 the gradient shrinks geometrically, above 1 it grows.

Exploding is loud — loss spikes, then NaN — and the fix is numerical: gradient clipping by global norm, one line. Vanishing is silent and far more dangerous: training appears to succeed while the model learns only short-range structure. The fix is architectural: gating (LSTM/GRU), plus orthogonal initialisation, layer norm and skip connections. Saying “numerical problem vs architectural problem” is what earns the point.

⭐ Walk through the LSTM gates. How exactly does the cell state fix vanishing gradients?

Forget f_t = σ(W_f·[h_{t-1},x_t]) — how much of the old cell state to keep. Input i_t — how much new content to write. Candidate C̃_t = tanh(...) — what that content is. Then C_t = f_t ⊙ C_{t-1} + i_t ⊙ C̃_t. Output o_t, and h_t = o_t ⊙ tanh(C_t). Sigmoid for gates (a 0–1 valve), tanh for content (a signed signal).

The mechanism: the cell state is updated additively and gated elementwise, so the gradient path backward along C is multiplied only by f_t — not by a weight matrix. With the forget gate near 1, gradient magnitude is preserved over hundreds of steps. That path is the constant error carousel. Parameter count: 4 × ((d_in + d_h)·d_h + d_h).

What is the seq2seq bottleneck, how did attention fix it, and what is exposure bias?

Bottleneck: the encoder compresses the entire input into one fixed-size context vector, so quality degrades as input length grows — visible as a falling BLEU-vs-length curve. Attention lets the decoder read all encoder states, weighted per output step: c_t = Σ α_{t,i} h_i with α = softmax(score). Bahdanau's score is additive (vᵀtanh(W₁h+W₂s)), Luong's multiplicative (sᵀWh) — the latter is a matmul, which is why it's the ancestor of QKᵀ.

Exposure bias: during training the decoder is fed gold prefixes (teacher forcing); at inference it is fed its own predictions, so one early error compounds into a distribution it never trained on. Scheduled sampling is a partial fix. And add the caveat: attention weights look like alignments and help debugging, but attention is not explanation.

⭐ Explain BPE — training and then encoding.

Training: start with the corpus split into characters. Count all adjacent symbol pairs, merge the most frequent pair into a single symbol, append it to an ordered merge list, and repeat until you hit the target vocabulary size. Encoding: split the input into characters and replay the learned merges in order, greedily — which makes it deterministic.

Details that show depth: the merge order is the model (a different order gives different tokens); pre-tokenization on whitespace/punctuation happens first; and byte-level BPE (GPT-2 onward) runs over the 256 raw bytes so any Unicode string is representable with zero UNK — at the cost of non-Latin characters consuming several tokens each.

BPE vs WordPiece vs Unigram vs SentencePiece.

Direction and criterion. BPE and WordPiece both merge upward; Unigram prunes downward from a large candidate vocabulary using EM plus loss-based pruning. BPE merges by raw pair frequency; WordPiece merges by likelihood, scoring roughly freq(AB)/(freq(A)·freq(B)), so it prefers informative merges over merely common ones. Unigram keeps a probability per piece, so it can return the most probable segmentation or sample one — which enables subword regularization (BPE-dropout is the BPE equivalent).

SentencePiece is not an algorithm — it is a framework implementing BPE and Unigram whose real contribution is treating input as a raw byte/character stream with whitespace encoded as ▁. That makes it pre-tokenizer-free, exactly reversible, and usable for Chinese, Japanese and Thai. Users: GPT/Llama/RoBERTa (BPE), BERT/ELECTRA (WordPiece), T5/ALBERT/XLNet and most multilingual models (Unigram via SentencePiece).

Why can't a model count the r's in “strawberry”, and why is your Hindi app 3× the token cost?

Both are tokenizer artefacts, not reasoning failures. “strawberry” is two or three opaque subword tokens — the model has no view of the individual letters, so character counting, string reversal and rhyming are structurally hard. (Related: inconsistent digit grouping is a genuine cause of arithmetic errors, which is why some tokenizers split digits individually.)

Token premium: under a byte-level BPE trained mostly on English, non-Latin scripts consume several tokens per character. The same content therefore costs more money, more latency, and gets a smaller effective context — for those users only. Measure it with characters-per-token and fertility. Framing it as simultaneously a cost, latency and fairness issue is what makes this a senior answer.

Your NER labels are misaligned after tokenization. What went wrong, and what breaks at eval time?

Training side: one word became several sub-tokens and the word-level labels were applied positionally without alignment. The fix is to use word_ids() (or offset_mapping): assign the label to the first sub-token of each word and mask continuations with -100, PyTorch's ignore_index, so they contribute no loss. Special tokens get -100 too.

Evaluation side — the mirror-image bug: you must re-aggregate sub-token predictions into word-level spans before computing entity-level F1 with seqeval. Scoring at the sub-token level measures a different task and usually flatters the model. Neither bug raises an error, which is exactly why they're common. Also note: in transformers v5 there is no slow/fast tokenizer choice — offset_mapping and word_ids() are always available.

Would you ever ship an RNN in 2026?

For text, rarely — a transformer encoder or decoder wins on quality, parallelism and ecosystem. Legitimate exceptions: very small models on embedded hardware, and strict streaming where you need O(1) state per step rather than a growing KV cache.

Then the part that shows range: the concepts came back. A fixed-size recurrent state with constant per-token inference cost is exactly what Mamba and the SSM family revived, for the original reason — attention's cost grows with sequence length and a recurrence's does not. The 2026 framing is not “RNNs are obsolete” but “recurrence returned with better mathematics, and production models now interleave it with attention”.
